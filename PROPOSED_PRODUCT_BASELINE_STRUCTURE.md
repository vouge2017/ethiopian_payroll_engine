## Three planning levels

| Level | Governs | Approval/evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Product North Star | Long-term ambition and non-negotiable principles. | Management endorsement; remains separate from committed scope. |
| Approved Product Baseline | Customer, problems, approved capabilities, release boundaries, responsibilities and measurable outcomes. | Recorded management decision, customer evidence, legal/provider review where applicable. |
| Delivery Roadmap | Sequenced engineering work, dependencies, owners and acceptance evidence. | Product/technical approval, current implementation evidence and available capacity. |

## Proposed sections for a future baseline

1. Document owner, version, approval record and change history.
2. Product purpose, North Star reference, boundaries and explicit non-goals.
3. Initial customer segment, buyer, users and validated customer evidence.
4. Customer problems, current workflow and measurable value hypotheses.
5. Approved launch scope and deferred capabilities.
6. End-to-end journeys: business setup, employee records, payroll inputs, calculation, review, approval, outputs, payment handoff and correction.
7. Roles, company-scoped permissions, approval rules and responsibility matrix.
8. Ethiopian statutory calculation principles, source governance, effective dates and professional approval records.
9. Filing/payment responsibility boundaries and allowed evidence states.
10. Integrations: required first-phase imports/exports versus candidate future adapters; provider access evidence.
11. Language requirements, terminology review and accessibility.
12. Privacy, hosting, subprocessors, data minimization, retention, export and closure requirements.
13. Reliability: saving, synchronization, backup, restoration, error handling and support expectations.
14. Product quality attributes and measurable targets for the defined pilot envelope.
15. Commercial model assumptions and research evidence; no price claim without validation.
16. Pilot stages, entry/exit gates, customer consent and success measures.
17. Operational support model, incident handling and customer responsibilities.
18. Release criteria, known limitations, sign-offs and rollback/stop conditions.
19. Traceability from each approved need to PRD, architecture decision, implementation, test and evidence.
20. Assumptions, unresolved questions, external approvals and reopening triggers.

## Baseline acceptance rule

Each approved requirement must identify its user/problem, scope, owner, measurable acceptance criteria, downstream dependencies, legal/provider evidence if relevant, data/security impact, release phase and sign-off. A roadmap item or historical PRD does not become a baseline requirement by mention alone.
